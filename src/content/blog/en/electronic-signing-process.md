A signing request does not begin when someone draws a signature on a screen. First, the document must be prepared, its recipients selected and the process controls defined. Then the parties should be able to review what happened and retrieve the result.

## 1. Prepare the document and participants

Start by choosing the PDF to be signed. The sender adds the people who need to take part and places the signature and data fields required on the document.

Check names and email addresses before sending the request. An incorrect recipient can expose information or delay the process. If the same document is used often, a [document template](/en/features/document-templates/) with variables can reduce repeated data entry.

## 2. Define reviews and their order

Some documents go straight to signing. Others need internal approval first, for example from a legal team or the person responsible for a budget.

An [approval workflow](/en/features/approvals-and-workflows/) can assign reviews sequentially or in parallel. Rules clarify whether one approval is enough, whether a majority is needed or whether everyone assigned must approve. Set those rules before starting so each participant knows what is expected.

## 3. Send and follow requests

Once the envelope is ready, each signer receives an email invitation. From its link, they can review the document, complete assigned fields and confirm their signature. Depending on organization policy, the process may also require a passcode or a one-time code by email.

The sender can check envelope status and see which actions are still pending. Clear follow-up helps address delays without circulating different copies of the same file.

## 4. Retrieve the document and its records

When the process is complete, the final document can be downloaded with a completion certificate. The certificate groups available file and process data, such as hashes, verification method and recorded events.

The [verification portal](/en/features/traceability-and-compliance/) lets someone check hashes and metadata associated with the certificate code. It does not show the document contents.

## A note on legal effect

Electronic signatures do not necessarily have the same requirements or effects in every situation. The answer depends on the document, the parties and applicable jurisdiction. Technical records help reconstruct a process but do not replace legal review when a document requires it.

A good signing flow makes its steps, owners and outcome visible. Carefully preparing the document and defining verification before sending usually prevents more issues than adding steps afterward.
